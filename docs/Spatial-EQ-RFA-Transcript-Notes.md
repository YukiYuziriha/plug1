# RFA Spatial EQ: technical transcript notes

**Source:** The user-supplied transcript of Yousef from RFA explaining RFA Spatial EQ. The speaker says the free EQ was released the previous day. The transcript supplies neither a dated source link nor a plug-in version. This is a record of the explanation, not verification of the plug-in or a specification for HEAVEN. Quotes below are short transcript fragments; speech-to-text wording may be imperfect.

## Claims in transcript order

1. **Depth is a per-band control.** Yousef calls it the “third control” on every band. He does not give its units, default, parameter curve, or interactions with other controls.
2. **Ordinary mid/side EQ is the contrast.** It adjusts existing Mid or Side content. Raising Side on a mono vocal leaves it mono: with identical L/R, there is no difference signal to raise. The claim is that boosts with Depth do more than this, not that the plug-in never uses M/S internally.
3. **Boosts generate new stereo information.** Depth takes the part of the signal the band covers and “builds a whole new stereo picture around it.” The speaker says it works on “every mono sound.” This is a broad claim, not a guarantee of audible width at every setting or frequency; he later describes bass protection. Zero-gain behavior is not stated.
4. **Cuts are different.** With a cut and Depth **all the way up**, it acts “a lot like a side EQ”: “the middle comes out identical,” while the cut affects the sides **and the space other bands made**. He agrees that the M/S analogy is closer for cuts, not for boosts. He does not define the Mid comparison reference or tolerance, the behavior at partial Depth, or how the cut reaches other bands’ space.
5. **Cue 1 — Width.** Depth does five things together, framed as cues the ears use to judge distance. Identical L/R puts the sound in the middle; slightly different versions spread it out. Depth makes those versions of **the selected band**, which “stays in the same level.” That wording does not define a level metric: Mid level, each channel’s level, stereo power, peaks, and loudness are different things.
6. **Cue 2 — Short, delayed reflections.** Reflections are later arrivals: the direct sound arrives first, then the reflected sound “a moment later.” The listener does not hear discrete echoes; the arrivals fuse, and the first arrival supplies the direction cue. More reflected sound **relative to direct sound** suggests greater distance. He describes this as putting walls around **that band**. A short delay between arrivals is part of the explanation; it is not a disclosure of a delay-line implementation, tap count, timing, feedback, or room geometry.
7. **Cue 3 — Darkening the generated space.** He uses air absorbing high frequencies as the distance analogy: the further back it goes, the darker the space. This acts **only on the space**; “your band keeps its own top.” No filter type, cutoff, slope, or mapping from Depth to damping is provided.
8. **Cue 4 — Transient protection.** On a hit, “the space steps back for a moment” so the attack stays close and clean. The stated target is the space, not the direct attack. The detector signal, threshold, attack/recovery timing, and whether it is broadband or per-band are unspecified.
9. **Cue 5 — Low-frequency protection.** He says the low end “never” gets space, so bass stays in the middle, to protect against phasing issues. No cutoff, transition slope, or leakage floor is stated. The transcript does not establish whether it also narrows *pre-existing* stereo bass or only avoids generating new low-frequency space.
10. **Reverb-like, but mostly width.** Asked if it is reverb, he says “closer”: there are reflections, but it “barely” has a tail, more like a very small room than a hall. Most of what the listener hears is width. The space is local to the band and moves when the band moves. No decay time, wet ratio, sharp frequency boundary, or behavior during automation is disclosed. This describes a short room-like component, not zero tail and not a long reverb wash.
11. **DIY approximation, not a schematic.** He suggests splitting off the “bend” (apparently *band*), adding a widener, reverb, EQ, and something to duck it, with a lot of routing. He says this gets “really close” and that the plug-in simplifies the workflow. This list is not a disclosed signal chain, processing order, or claim that those specific processors are used internally.
12. **Mono behavior.** He contrasts it with wideners that sound large in stereo but fall apart on a phone. “Almost everything” Depth adds lives in the L/R difference, so **that part** cancels on mono fold-down; what remains is “basically just your EQ.” The claimed listening result is loss of depth rather than a hollow, phasey sound. For an equal-weight fold, `(L + R) / 2`, the Side component `(L - R) / 2` is discarded by definition; any Depth-dependent Mid change survives. The wording does not promise an exact null or that another processor between the EQ and fold preserves cancellation.
13. **Reported number: one vocal, two Depth settings.** On the demonstrated vocal, he says the **mono** result at **full Depth** is within **0.2 dB at every frequency** of the **mono** result at **Depth off**. The transcript provides no frequency bounds, settings, analysis bins, smoothing, floor, alignment, or comparison method. This is a reported level-by-frequency comparison, not a sample-wise null, phase test, or specification across all material and settings. In particular, do not extrapolate this comparison to maximum-Depth cuts: if a cut leaves Mid alone at full Depth but changes it at Depth off, the two mono results can differ by design.
14. **Mono input, stereo output.** The vocal demonstrated was mono. The speaker says the output has to be stereo because Depth lives in the difference between the two speakers. This explains the signal requirement to hear the effect; it does not enumerate plug-in bus layouts or host routing requirements.
15. **Closing recap and feature list.** The recap restates width, reflections, darker top on the space, backing off hits, centered bass, and mono folding back to the EQ. He names eight bands plus “a dynamic, midside, and linear phase.” Whether the latter are global modes, per-band options, or usable in combination with Depth is not stated.

## What to measure before choosing a DSP design

These are proposed measurements, **not results** or claims about RFA’s internals. Record the plug-in build, sample rate, I/O, latency, settings, stimulus, and analysis conventions so later comparisons are repeatable.

| Question | Measurement |
| --- | --- |
| What creates new Side, and where? | Use identical L/R signals and compare Depth off, intermediate, and maximum across boost, zero gain, and cut. Sweep band frequency and Q with in-band and out-of-band tones or noise; inspect Mid, Side, per-channel level, total stereo power, and correlation separately. |
| What does a deep cut affect? | Use stereo input with known Mid and Side content. Compare no cut, Depth-off cut, and maximum-Depth cut. Repeat with an overlapping boost generating Side from dual mono and with non-overlapping controls. This separates input Side from other bands’ generated space and tests what “identical” means. |
| How short are the reflections and any tail? | Compare L/R and Mid/Side impulse responses with Depth off/on; inspect direct arrival, later energy and time to the measurement floor. Repeat with band-limited bursts and a sustained signal followed by silence. Band filters can ring and transient ducking can change an impulse response; a peak or short decay alone does not identify a delay tap or reverb topology. Listen for fused reflections versus distinct repeats. |
| Does the space darken, duck, and leave bass alone? | Sweep the spectrum of the Depth-induced change and compare Mid against the same EQ with Depth off. Measure Side energy around isolated hits at different levels and through recovery, including hits over a sustained signal. Sweep the low end on dual mono and already-stereo input to distinguish lack of added Side from removal of existing Side. |
| Does mono match, and in which cases? | Hold EQ settings fixed, align latency, and compare equal-weight mono folds by spectral magnitude, waveform residual, and listening. Start with a mono vocal and repeat with controlled signals and cut/boost cases. Report the frequency span and analysis floor rather than treating the quoted 0.2 dB as a universal target. |
| What does the UI actually allow? | Inventory band types, Depth range/default and the dynamic, M/S, and linear-phase controls; test availability in combination with Depth and measure latency separately. |

Keep exact tap times, decay, filtering, topology, band order, and Depth mapping out of the source-claim record unless independent evidence supports them. Record measurements and candidate explanations separately; matching the sound does not identify a unique implementation.
